# Vision

## Mission

Build **Power & Proximity** as an original, expandable social-systems game where consequential human behavior can be modeled once and expressed across many industries, institutions, and intimate settings.

The project should become a complete experience rather than a thin layer over another game’s engine. Each new content pack should deepen the shared simulation instead of creating a disconnected game.

## Product promise

Players should be able to understand and strategically use:

- who knows whom;
- how close each actor is to power;
- what each actor values and can do;
- what stories precede them;
- how trust, resentment, gratitude, fear, and legitimacy accumulate;
- how a small choice becomes a social or institutional consequence.

The game should support both public and private pressure:

- a dishwasher deciding how to respond to a health violation;
- a manager choosing between staff solidarity and business survival;
- an activist, lobbyist, and politician pursuing the same issue through different tools;
- a journalist entering a new sector with a controversial story attached;
- a remote worker deciding whether to meet a deadline or attend to a pet.

Small interactions and structural conflicts must use the same causal foundation.

## Core experience pillars

### 1. Power through proximity

Formal authority is only one kind of power. An actor may have little money or status but possess moral authority, technical credibility, trusted relationships, or access to a crucial bridge person.

### 2. Legible ripples

A choice should produce a clear outcome and a costly tradeoff. Its effect should propagate through relevant edges rather than changing every character arbitrarily.

The player should be able to inspect why a reaction occurred:

- direct harm or benefit;
- issue alignment;
- trait disposition;
- relationship trust;
- remembered history;
- role pressure;
- story interpretation;
- current scarcity or stress.

### 3. Continuous character formation

Actors begin with stable coordinates on shared spectra, but repeated behavior can shift dispositions, habits, self-concept, relationships, and reputation.

NPCs should remain learnable enough for strategic play. Determinism is the baseline; bounded seeded variation adds texture without erasing causality.

### 4. Universal substrate, portable packs

Politics, restaurants, gyms, research labs, public safety, technology companies, home offices, and future domains use the same actor, graph, memory, reputation, and consequence contracts.

A pack may provide:

- domain aliases;
- roles and organizations;
- issue vectors;
- pressures and resources;
- scenarios and choices;
- narrative templates;
- conversion rules;
- domain-specific capabilities.

A pack must not silently replace the human engine.

### 5. Stories instead of a universal reputation bar

Fame and infamy are not opposite endpoints of one score.

A story has reach, clarity, controversy, recency, and audience-specific interpretation. When actors move across domains, recognition events can surface their history:

> “Oh, you’re the person who did that.”

### 6. Institutional consequence without moral decoration

Trust, legitimacy, truth quality, merit integrity, and fairness must create operational benefits. Coercion, opacity, favoritism, metric gaming, and shortcuts may produce immediate gain but create debt.

The game should make both paths mechanically tempting for different reasons.

## First bounded proof

The first vertical slice is **Blue Corner Diner**, a restaurant and commercial corridor.

The slice should prove:

- persistent actors and relationship edges;
- continuous traits and capabilities;
- aliases such as gravitas becoming floor authority;
- low-power influence paths;
- intermediate choices rather than binary morality menus;
- memories and relationship changes;
- story creation and audience interpretation;
- one cross-domain recognition path;
- deterministic replay from a fixed seed.

## Replit-first principle

The project should remain easy to run, inspect, remix, and demonstrate in a browser-friendly development environment.

This principle does not yet mandate a specific framework. It requires:

- minimal setup;
- explicit commands;
- portable fixtures;
- inspectable schemas;
- deterministic sample runs;
- no hidden cloud dependency for core simulation;
- documentation sufficient for another agent or contributor to continue safely.

## Success criteria for the foundation phase

The foundation succeeds when the repository contains:

1. durable product and safety doctrine;
2. a coherent v0 schema;
3. a bounded first-pack contract;
4. deterministic formula and graph test targets;
5. a gaps and risks ledger;
6. clean branch and PR evidence;
7. no accidental commitment to a framework before the runtime sprint evaluates it.
